Design drivers — the creative constraints that shaped each iteration
🎯Tap ergonomics
Reader angled and raised so the tap target is intuitive and comfortable to reach.
🌬️Thermal venting
Side slots keep the Pi 3B cool across a full school day of use.
🔌Cable routing
Power and USB hidden internally, exiting through one tidy rear port.
💡Feedback visibility
A light pipe carries the "tap accepted" LED up to the top surface.
🛡️Student durability
Thick walls and rounded corners survive daily handling by students.
🎨Visual identity
A circular tap zone carries the school crest as a branded focal point.
Materials explored
Cardboard mockup Prototype
Built first — free and fast. Validated tap height and footprint in minutes before any digital modelling.
3D-printed PLA Chosen
Cheap and highly iterable — lets me print custom vent slots and the internal light-pipe channel.
Laser-cut acrylic Deferred
Premium finish, but curved surfaces and the light pipe are difficult to form from flat sheet.
Ongoing Evaluation

Sketching the enclosure on paper let me explore its form and ergonomics for free before committing filament to a 3D print. Each iteration deliberately solved a problem the previous one exposed — a sliding reader, then exposed wiring, then a hidden status LED — so the final dock is the product of refinement rather than a first guess. The next step is to model the refined dock in Fusion 360 and print a test fit to confirm vent placement and light-pipe alignment.
